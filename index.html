The idea

Your library shouldn't
lock you into one device.

Streaming services make music incredibly convenient. But many listeners still build and maintain their own local music libraries — and want the freedom to use the players and devices they choose.

Keep your music local

Your audio files stay in your own libraries. MusicShareProtocol coordinates playback state, not audio delivery.

Move between devices

Start listening on one device and continue on another player using its own local copy of the track.

Keep players independent

No replacement player or mandatory ecosystem. The goal is interoperability between applications that already exist.

The idea in one sentence: independent music players, sharing one listening session over your local network.

How it works

From discovery
to handoff.

The intended experience is simple for listeners. Underneath, participating players coordinate a shared session.

01

Discover peers

Players find other compatible devices on the local network.

02

Share session state

Participants exchange the current track, playback status, position and active device.

03

Hand off playback

The receiving player locates the matching local file and resumes from the shared position.

04

Surface conflicts

If participants disagree about the session, the protocol makes that conflict visible instead of silently choosing a winner.

The device diagram illustrates the intended experience, not a claim of universal device support. Compatibility depends on player integrations. The protocol and integrations are actively evolving.
